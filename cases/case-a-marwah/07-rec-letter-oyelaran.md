# Letter of Recommendation — Prof. Adaeze Oyelaran

Coastland State University
Department of Environmental Informatics

12 May 2026

To Whom It May Concern:

I am a Professor of Environmental Informatics at Coastland State University,
where I have taught since 2008. My own research concerns remote sensing for
land-use classification. I supervised Dr. Ishani Marwah's postdoctoral work from
2019 to 2022 and have co-authored three papers with her, including the ICVAA
2023 paper that received that conference's Best Paper Award.

I want to be direct about what I think is distinctive in her work, because I
have written enough of these letters to know that superlatives are cheap.

When Ishani arrived, the agricultural vision literature had a quiet problem
everyone knew about and nobody addressed: published segmentation results were
almost universally evaluated within a single growing region, and the models did
not transfer. Practitioners knew this. The field kept publishing anyway, because
the alternative was collecting annotation in multiple regions, which nobody
could afford.

Her contribution was to attack the annotation cost directly rather than to work
around the transfer problem. *Lyra* trains from sparse point annotations instead
of dense masks. This sounds incremental and is not: it changed what data a
research group needs in order to work in this area at all. The 2022 cross-region
paper then demonstrated the transfer result that the sparse-annotation approach
made affordable to test.

I would characterize the 2021 paper as the most consequential single piece of
work to come out of my lab in the last decade, and I did not write it — she did.
My contribution was arguing with her about the evaluation protocol.

The number I find most telling is not her citation count but the fact that two
agricultural agencies run her method operationally. In this field the distance
between a published result and an agency willing to bet its inspection schedule
on your output is enormous, and very few researchers cross it.

I should note plainly that I am not a disinterested observer: I was her
supervisor and I am her co-author. I would encourage you to weigh the enclosed
letter from Dr. Sunde, who has no such relationship with her, more heavily than
mine on questions of independent recognition. What I can speak to that he cannot
is the origin of the ideas, and on that point I am unambiguous — they were hers.

Sincerely,
