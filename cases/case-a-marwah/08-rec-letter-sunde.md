# Letter of Recommendation — Dr. Halvard Sunde

Cascadia Regional Agricultural Board
Office of the Chief Scientist

6 May 2026

To Whom It May Concern:

I am Chief Scientist at the Cascadia Regional Agricultural Board, a public body
responsible for crop health monitoring across roughly 2.1 million acres. I have
held this post since 2017. Before that I spent eleven years as a plant
pathologist with the state agricultural extension service.

I have never met Dr. Marwah in person, have never collaborated with her, and
have no professional or personal relationship with her beyond what I describe
below. I am writing because her method changed how my agency operates and I was
asked whether I would say so.

In 2023 we were drowning in imagery. We fly roughly 40,000 acres of drone survey
per season and we had no way to triage it. Inspectors were assigned to fields on
a rotation, which meant we found outbreaks when the rotation happened to reach
them. We evaluated four automated approaches, three commercial and one academic.

The academic one was *Lyra*. It was the only one that worked on our data without
us first assembling an annotation set we could not afford. That is the practical
difference her sparse-annotation approach makes, and I do not think people
outside operational settings appreciate how decisive it is. The commercial
vendors quoted us six-figure annotation contracts before they would commit to an
accuracy figure.

We put *Lyra* into production for the 2024 season. Our median time from
infection onset to inspector arrival went from 19 days to 6. I want to be careful
here: that figure comes from our internal retrospective and reflects our
particular workflow, not a controlled study. But the direction is not
ambiguous and my inspectors will tell you the same thing.

What persuaded me to write rather than decline was the calibration work. Dr.
Marwah's 2024 paper on uncertainty for operational advisory systems addressed
precisely the thing that makes agencies like mine refuse to adopt research
models: we cannot act on a detector that is confidently wrong. She treated that
as the central problem rather than an afterthought, which in my experience of
reading this literature for fifteen years is rare.

I understand the Sonoran Valley authority adopted the same method for a
different crop mix in 2025. I have no involvement in that deployment.

I am glad to answer questions.

Sincerely,
